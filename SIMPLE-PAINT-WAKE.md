# High Paint: advected disturbance wake

This report describes the retained particle prototype. The current independent [GPU field experiment](SIMPLE-PAINT-GPU-FLUID.md) is selected by `quality=high`; use `highBackend=particles` to compare this prototype explicitly.

This supersedes the cursor-local density pass. Standard remains the safe/default Canvas renderer. No authored path, musical, timing, note-generation, control, project persistence or Simple Paint input/state-flow implementation changed.

1. **Disturbance representation.** A separate `Float32Array` stores a Lagrangian disturbance scalar for each of the existing 1,800 material parcels (900 coarse pointer). Density and velocity remain liquid properties. Rendering reconstructs the disturbance field at current parcel positions. This is the lightweight equivalent of an advected energy texture: it follows the fluid rather than stamping a static screen trail. No extra particle budget or full fluid solver was added.

2. **Injection.** Only an actual moving-circle displacement deposits energy. Deposit strength uses penetration depth, forward normal approach and world pointer speed. Initial placement, a stationary finger, velocity alone and recycled calm material cannot illuminate the medium. The pressure band is narrowed to 18 world units around the physical repulsor. The large cursor rim has zero opacity; only the tiny cursor marker remains.

3. **Advection/decay.** Disturbance travels with particle motion, including velocity matching, radial pressure and spring-driven return flow. Energy decays exponentially with a 1.05-second time constant, versus 0.45 seconds previously. Strong disturbance is readable for roughly 1–2 seconds and then fades. Material rest positions stay anchored in the world instead of translating with the cursor. Disturbed parcels are protected from cold-distance recycling. Calm parcels outside 185 world units can refill the local medium; a 1,100-world-unit safety bound prevents unbounded retained wakes at extreme input speeds.

4. **Density pipeline.** One accumulation pass packs density, disturbance, velocity and pressure into separate RGBA channels, followed by two blur passes and an edge/flow composite. Body, edge and motion brightness are all gated by disturbance. There is no pointer-distance visibility term or particle-core pass. The world-aligned texture domain encloses the retained material, including old wakes. Texture resolution derives from world extent at approximately two world units per texel, rounded into power-of-two buckets and capped at 512 per axis. Allocations grow only during an effect, preventing texture churn as bounds move. Resolution is independent of viewport, zoom and DPR. Aspect correction preserves world geometry when the texture axes use different sampling rates.

5. **Channel closure.** Removing cursor-relative rest-target translation lets particles flow back toward their original material positions after passage. Rest stiffness is 18/s², neighbour velocity matching 10/s, and damping 6/s; speed remains capped at 105 world units/s. A test samples the channel behind a two-second straight gesture and verifies that its particle population increases by more than 30% during subsequent settling, with the same pool and full opacity. Closure therefore includes actual inward density movement. Release injects no energy and no longer starts an immediate whole-field fade: it waits for low disturbance and low velocity, with a 6.5-second emergency cleanup ceiling. Browser checks reached zero active particles after the wake settled.

6. **Edge/flow lighting.** Density samples on either side of a texel estimate a normalized gradient. Disturbance gates the stronger edge highlight; the interior is darker/softer. Velocity stretches density splats by at most 35%, adding directional structure inside the liquid without discrete comet trails. Blur is reduced from 4.5 to 3 world units to retain channel detail.

7. **Colour.** The interior retains the instrument colour. Strong boundaries and the compact pressure front become pale, while the shoulder remains coloured. Whitening is edge/pressure-led rather than a broad pastel body. The GPU pixel test measured zero alpha for fast but undisturbed material, alpha 77 in the disturbed interior, and 155 at its stronger boundary, with no GL error.

8. **Particle prominence.** Individual cores remain disabled. The density field carries the shape; no emitted particles, long streaks, release burst or separate sparkle system was introduced.

9. **Refraction.** Deferred. The board is composed from DOM and Canvas layers rather than a safely available background texture. Sampling/rebuilding the whole scene for this effect would expand the scope and cost; the shader does not simulate a fake full-screen lens.

10. **Performance.** Same CPU benchmark, 120 warmup plus 600 measured frames: High before mean 0.841 ms, median 0.780 ms, p95 1.073 ms; final High mean 1.671 ms, median 1.481 ms, p95 2.224 ms. Runs varied around 1.5–1.8 ms mean. Retained wakes require a sparse fixed-world 16-unit cell grid instead of the former pointer-clamped 28×28 grid. Warm browser samples after stabilization were around 1.8–2.7 ms simulation and 0.12–0.34 ms render submission. Cold/load-affected samples were substantially higher and sometimes exceeded a frame budget; no GPU-completion or mobile performance guarantee is established. The three RGBA8 textures use at most 3 MiB, excluding the main drawing buffer and compositor. RGBA8 avoids optional float-render/blend extensions but can lose low-energy detail or saturate dense peaks.

11. **Browser observations.** The production side-by-side fixture exercised straight slow, straight fast, pause, sharp turn, loop, release and 50%/200% zoom. Fast movement left a long edge-lit groove several finger diameters behind the compact front, rather than a circular cloud moving with the pointer. Old material remained visible after passage. Curved gestures retained a curved wake and closing edges; strong resolved eddies are not established by this approximation. A paused finger went dark with no surrounding halo. Release continued settling rather than instantly hiding the field, then shut down. World scaling and pointer alignment remained intact. Standard retained its original appearance. The Fast fixture is now a one-way straight gesture so wake length/closure are easier to compare, and diagnostics show the sparse flow-cell count and local density-buffer size.

12. **Assessment/limits.** The dominant read is now a finger leaving an illuminated liquid groove, especially at moderate/fast speed. Slow movement is deliberately more compact. Curling is subtle and spring/grid-driven, not full vorticity dynamics. Very long or extreme gestures remain constrained by pool/domain budgets, and low-energy far wake can become sparse. These limits are reported rather than hidden by a premade capsule, rotating trail or extra particles.

# Simple Paint: distance fade and persistent hot wake

1. DrawGrid radius reuse: added src/drawgrid/drawgrid-pointer-radius.js, extracting DrawGrid's existing +50% effective-radius calculation. Normal pointer input and the ghost guide now use that helper without changing their radius formulas. Paint derives its screen reference from createDGTuning and DrawGrid's normal 800 x 600 logical size: min(800,600) * 0.054 * 1.5 = 48.6. The quarter-body radius cap does not reduce this normal value.
2. Effective radius: 48.6 CSS px, matching normal 1:1 DrawGrid interaction, with a faint outline of the same radius. Paint keeps this CSS size fixed under board zoom and DPR by converting world offsets into screen lengths before force evaluation. The 5 px safety core remains tiny. Pure radial cubic repulsion remains; base acceleration was reduced from 16,000 to 6,000 px/s^2 for the wider area, retaining the existing maximum 25% scalar speed boost. No bow, forward wedge or side/path steering was added.
3. Calm distance fade: full base visibility through 72.9 CSS px (1.5 times the shared repulsion radius), then 1 - smoothstep across 72.9-160 px, reaching zero at 160 px. This is smooth at both ends. Actual particle distance, not age or pointer speed, controls this calm-field fade.
4. Recent-push state: each pool particle has transient pushEnergy, peakPushEnergy, an impulse accumulator, first meaningful push time and bounded wake expiry. Only radial accelerations of at least 300 px/s^2 contribute to the accumulator. It must reach 6 px/s of impulse to register a meaningful push; incidental outer-edge forces do not heat particles. Registered energy is impulse/65, clamped to 0.12-1. Repeated pushes preserve position and velocity.
5. Energy decay and visibility: pushEnergy decays exponentially with a 650 ms half-life. Wake visibility blends the distance fade toward full visibility using smoothstep(pushEnergy / 0.35). High-energy particles therefore ignore the calm distance fade; cooling particles gradually rejoin it. Reduced motion uses a 90 ms half-life, minimal contact energy and no distance-fade bypass.
6. Colour-to-white: the core perceptually blends the current instrument colour toward white in OKLab, beginning above pushEnergy 0.15 and reaching approximately 84% white at maximum heat. The small quantized colour cache avoids reparsing every continuous energy value. Glow and streak always retain the base instrument colour. Cooling returns the core toward that colour. Reduced motion has no white-hot blend or long streaks.
7. Lifetime: meaningful pushes add up to 900 ms to the existing 900-1500 ms disturbed-particle lifetime, scaled by peak push strength. The absolute ceiling is anchored to the first meaningful push; repeated weak contacts cannot accumulate the full extension or create an immortal particle. Calm release fade remains 420 ms; pushed release tails can gain up to 900 ms, for a maximum 1320 ms, additionally limited by their wake expiry. This permits the new persistent wake to outlive the old global release cutoff. Reduced contact persistence is capped at 180 ms and receives no extra release tail.
8. Velocity lighting: retained the shared DrawGrid highlight appearance. Brightness uses max(velocityEnergy, pushEnergy), where velocityEnergy is the existing smoothstep of actual particle speed / 320 px/s. A recently pushed particle can remain luminous while slowing, and a fast unheated particle still receives velocity lighting. Core size, glow and capped 12 px streaks remain bounded.
9. Pool/recycling: unchanged budgets of 96 desktop, 48 coarse pointer and 8 reduced motion. Calm, distant, low-energy particles can recycle once beyond the zero-visibility boundary. Hot wake particles are protected from distance-only recycling until they cool or reach their bounded expiry. The canvas covers the local field plus visible hot wake, clipped to the viewport with quantized growth; the former fixed patch no longer clips a bright distant particle. All animation and the canvas stop when the tail is finished. No authored/project state receives particle data.
10. Browser comparison: the production-renderer fixture shows matching 48.6 px circles beside DrawGrid's production radial particle field. Slow movement showed a local medium with fading edges. Fast movement showed hotter, near-white cores with tinted halos and a wake beyond the calm field. Pause let the old wake coast/cool while calm particles remained distance-faded. Sharp turns retained the old hot wake alongside new contacts. A six-second fast scenario stayed at 96 particles. After release, the pushed canvas still existed at 0.5 seconds and was removed by 1.5 seconds. A real drag in the Simple Playtest app also created a Paint phrase and started transport normally.

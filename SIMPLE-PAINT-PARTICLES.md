# Simple Paint dense liquid tuning

1. Model: lightweight moving rest field, spatial-hash short-range pressure and neighbour velocity matching. No projectile ages, contact lifetime extensions, stored heat or artificial vortices. Rest targets translate with the local field; particles return gradually through damped springs.
2. Pool: 224 desktop, 112 coarse pointer, 24 reduced motion; original 130 CSS px distribution radius retained. Reduced motion stays static and fades on release.
3. Finger: existing DrawGrid shared helper, 48.6 CSS px, independent of zoom/DPR. A faint rim marks the displaced circle without a spotlight.
4. Pointer: radial acceleration 1100 px/s? times linear penetration, plus positional exclusion at the circle boundary. Positional correction never becomes launch velocity. Swept substeps prevent fast input from skipping the circle.
5. Damping: 0.86 per 60 Hz frame (previously 0.99); neighbour velocity matching coefficient 10/s. Spring coefficient 7/s?. Substeps are capped at 16 and frame dt at 33 ms.
6. Cohesion/pressure: rest springs bind particles to the medium. A 25 CSS px spatial hash averages nearby velocities; neighbours inside 13 px repel with up to 55 px/s? per neighbour. No deliberate front wedge or vortex force.
7. Speed: actual simulation clamp reduced from 720 to 110 CSS px/s. Input speed remains separately measured up to 900 px/s for diagnostics; fast displacement affects more particles without increasing launch speed.
8. Fade: full visibility through 95 px, smoothstep to zero at 160 px. No hot-particle bypass. Outliers beyond 185 px recycle into the local distribution. Canvas stays local and pointer-transparent.
9. Lighting: current speed drives brightness and glow; no recent-contact timer. Core white blend capped near 28%, glow retains instrument colour. Soft dots are 2.6?3.4 CSS px before highlight scaling, with 7?12 px tinted shadow blur.
10. Streak: previously up to 12 px; now only above 75 px/s and capped at 2 px.
11. Wake: circle exclusion opens a channel, springs refill it gradually, viscosity shares motion locally. Release removes exclusion and lets the final void close while the entire field fades over 1000 ms. No release burst or new settle-sparkle system.

Performance: warmed Node desktop simulation benchmark, 780 measured curved-motion frames after 120 warmup frames: mean 1.03 ms, median 0.97 ms, p95 1.40 ms, pool 224, about 11,192 candidate neighbour checks per frame. Browser fixture samples typically showed 1?2 ms simulation and 0.5?0.9 ms render submission, with scenario averages around 2?4 ms total; occasional higher samples occurred. Render timing measures synchronous canvas commands, not GPU completion. Mobile hardware remains unmeasured.
13. Browser observations: production renderer fixture checked slow, fast, pause, turn and release. Slow movement displaces a readable circular void within a dense field. Fast motion leaves material lagging behind without long isolated streaks or spray. Pause settles and dims around the circle. Release visibly fills the final hole and removes the field by one second. Turns preserve lag; convincing curled eddies are not established by this lightweight model. The appearance remains visibly particulate and needs subjective confirmation in normal play.
